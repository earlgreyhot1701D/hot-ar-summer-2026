The story so far

The Lakers won 53 games, took the 4 seed, and got swept out of the playoffs by Oklahoma City. Then the real season started. LeBron James declined to re-sign after 8 seasons in Los Angeles. Deandre Ayton was traded to Washington. The first five arrivals made the July board; Kevon Looney, Matisse Thybulle, and Ziaire Williams changed the roster math after it.

Every bet rides on one player: Luka Dončić, who led the NBA in 2025–26 usage and scoring. This Aug. 30 snapshot scores eight arrivals, the returning rotation, and only the affordable names still on the late-summer board. New-Laker scores remain projections; returning-Laker scores retain the stronger evidence of shared floor time.

The rules we scored by

Decided before the scores, so the scores couldn't bend them. The tool keeps a public record of correcting itself.
WHY USAGE?

The whole thesis

Usage rate is the share of team possessions a player ends — a shot, free throws, or a turnover. It's the cleanest measurable answer to "who has the ball?" Luka ended a higher share of team possessions than any NBA player in 2025–26, so every teammate's fit starts with one question: can you be great without it?

NEVER

No blended score

One composite number implies a verdict the data can't back. Four axes stay four axes. If you want a single number, this is the wrong tool on purpose.

AXES

Four measurable needs

Spacing (3P% + volume), play finishing (TS% without the ball), defensive cover (who guards so Luka doesn't), and ball-need — inverted: lower usage scores higher next to the league's highest-usage player.

MUST

Confidence tags are first-class

Sample size, shooting volume, team context, role, and injury status sit next to every score, not in a footnote. A bar without its context tag is an incomplete claim.

FOUND

The Luka rest problem

Season usage blends minutes next to Luka with minutes while he rests — opposite signals. Fix: with/without splits. Applying it softened the tool's own loudest take (see Reaves).

UPGRADED

Defense, second pass

Cover scores originally leaned on steals, blocks, and reputation. They now start with individual defensive rating — points allowed per 100 possessions on court. It remains box-derived and team-flavored, so reputation never overrides the number and team context stays visible.

LENS

Fit and need never merge

Fit asks "does his game complement Luka?" Need asks "does this current roster need his role?" Looney, Thybulle, and Williams materially changed the need answer without changing the fit axes. The two lenses stay separate.

RUBRIC

Editorial, not algorithmic

The bars are disclosed editorial ratings informed by the displayed statistic, role, and volume: 0 means the skill is absent; 1 poor; 2 below average; 3 workable; 4 strong; 5 elite for a Luka complement. Ball-need reverses usage. No arithmetic combines the axes, and context tags can lower confidence without secretly changing a bar.

How to read more gold = stronger fit vs Luka's needs (0–5) purple = fit flag Ball-need inverted: lower usage = more gold

Pattern found
Roster construction, not one player
The high-usage guard overlap remains: Reaves, Sexton, and Hardy all need creation possessions. But the low-usage connector group is no longer just Vanderbilt and LaRavia. Looney and Thybulle add defense without demanding the ball, while Williams adds real wing size. The tradeoff moved from pure scarcity to lineup compatibility: Looney, Thybulle, and Vanderbilt cannot all solve defense at once without squeezing spacing.
Verdict: the glue room grew; the guard overlap stayed. Thybulle changes the perimeter-defense conclusion, Looney changes the center-insurance conclusion, and Williams changes the wing-count conclusion. None removes the need to stagger Luka, Reaves, Sexton, and Hardy.
Development roster (not scored — small n by design): Bronny James, Adou Thiero, Cameron Carr.   Two-way: Chris Manon, AK Okereke, Peter Suder.   Roster snapshot: 15 standard contracts and three two-way contracts on Aug. 30.   Departed: LeBron James (declined to re-sign), Deandre Ayton (traded to Washington July 3).

Ch. 3 · Who to chase

The Lakers reached 15 standard contracts by Aug. 30, so this is no longer an open-roster-spot shopping list. It is a contingency board for a later transaction, restricted to two late-August names whose roles still make basketball sense.

Two lenses, never merged

Luka fit asks whether the player can space, finish, cover defensively, and work without the ball. Roster need asks what remains after Looney added center insurance, Thybulle added point-of-attack defense, and Williams added wing size. Those additions lowered urgency; they did not turn fit and need into one number.

The data

Every displayed statistic traces to the sources below. Ratings are human-reviewed editorial judgments, not a hidden formula, and context labels identify where a clean number would overstate certainty.
STATS

StatMuse, 2025–26

Player totals, shooting, true shooting, usage, defensive rating, and the Reaves availability splits come from StatMuse. Kessler alone uses his 2024–25 full-season line because the following season ended after five games.

ROSTER

NBA + Lakers + Spotrac

The Aug. 30 snapshot uses the official Lakers roster, Lakers announcements, and Spotrac's 2026 cap table as its transaction and contract source register.

NEW ARRIVALS

Same prior-team standard

Looney, Thybulle, and Williams use the same 2025–26 StatMuse categories as the July arrivals: 3P% with volume context, TS%, individual DRtg, USG%, games, role, and prior team. Their Lakers fit remains a projection until shared-floor data exists.

MARKET

Late-August transaction check

The active board was checked against the NBA free-agent tracker and team transaction reporting. Signed former targets remain visible with dates so the original July conclusion is corrected rather than erased.

METHOD

The 0–5 bars

The bars are editorial ratings, using the disclosed 0 absent / 1 poor / 2 below average / 3 workable / 4 strong / 5 elite rubric. Defensive cover begins with individual DRtg but retains a team-context warning. Ball-need reverses usage and uses with-Luka splits for returnees, season usage for arrivals. The four bars are never averaged.

About this project

The Luka Fit Index is a for-fun analytics experiment built in one brainstorming session: a bias-visibility tool disguised as a sports dashboard. The interesting part was never the scores — it's that the assumptions are printed on the page, and the tool corrected itself twice when the data disagreed with its takes.

Built PRD-first with a multi-LLM workflow. Deterministic structure, labeled interpretation, receipts kept in a spike file the whole way.

AI assisted. Human reviewed. Powered by NLP.

The honesty layer

Fit scores describe stat profiles against Luka Dončić's measurable needs. They cannot prove causality, predict chemistry, or account for lineups that have never shared a possession. Signing scores use prior-team context. Free agent scores use the same, at minimum-contract expectations.
